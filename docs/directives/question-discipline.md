# Question Discipline

**Purpose.** The operator is in class with a 5-minute standup budget and billed meetings. A drip of
foreseeable questions is expensive here in a way it isn't on most projects.

- **Run a question audit at the START of a work item.** Read ahead in scope/roadmap/plans and enumerate
  every decision only the operator can make: scope calls, anything irreversible or outward-facing
  (hub firmware, submissions, git), genuinely ambiguous requirements, trade-offs with no safe default.
- **Ask in ROUNDS.** Round 1 = everything answerable now, in ONE `AskUserQuestion` call. Round 2+ =
  only follow-ups that needed round 1's answers. Never a one-at-a-time drip; never a giant
  half-formed list.
- **Lead with a recommendation and a default** so it's one tap, not homework.
- **"Defer until X" is a command, not a dismissal.** Record the re-raise trigger in `docs/todo.md` and
  bring it back when X happens.
- **Stop when anchored.** Once the big decision is made, the small ones derive — decide them yourself.
- **Proceed on sensible defaults; halt only the blocked item.** A pending question freezes that item;
  disjoint work keeps moving.
- **Never guess the mission.** The design briefing is the one thing that cannot be defaulted. An
  unforeseen operator-only decision still halts that item and gets raised.
- **Things only the operator can answer here:** the design briefing · the arena (size, surface,
  boundary) · team roles and names · whether a Hub OS update is acceptable · anything submitted for
  a grade · the out-of-class-work ruling.
