## Summary of Major Events from Today

**DRAFT — repo-sourced. Review every sentence.**

The hub was connected for the first time in the project, and all six ports A–F read **empty** the
whole session because the robot had not been built yet. Reading the hub itself settled what generation
it is — **SPIKE 3, MicroPython 1.24.0, with no `spike` module** — which means every SPIKE 2 tutorial
online is inapplicable to this hub, and most of the material online is SPIKE 2. Getting code onto the
hub without the LEGO app was **proven**: 13,262 bytes uploaded in 3.6 seconds and verified by a
SHA-256 that the hub computed on itself, and the firmware was then **proved untouched** by capturing a
baseline before the upload and diffing it afterwards — every stock file byte-identical. Bluetooth was
brought up from Linux with raw `bleak`, and the hub was identified by matching its device UUID
`03970000-3600-1B00-1450-30514B323320` over **both** USB and Bluetooth rather than trusting its
advertised name. The most instructive result of the day was a **discarded** one: a 30-second gyro run
reported 98.7° of drift, which was thrown out because the drift reversed direction and steady drift
does not do that; the probe was rewritten to watch the accelerometer while it measured, it
independently flagged the run **CONTAMINATED** and refused to report a number, and the clean re-run
gave **≤0.0033 °/s**. Three answers about the mission were also relayed from the professor through a
teammate — *"we expect yellow"*, **no walls with the boundary marked in floor tape**, and an answer
about a human operator that **contradicts itself** and was therefore deliberately **not** recorded as
answered.
