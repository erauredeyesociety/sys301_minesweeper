## Summary of Major Events from Today

**DRAFT — repo-sourced. Review every sentence.**

The robot drove a **defined 1 foot × 1 foot square untethered on battery** — the program was uploaded
to a hub slot and started, the USB cable was pulled out, the robot drove four sides and four
gyro-closed 90° turns, and the log was retrieved afterwards: **238 rows over 25.8 seconds at about
9.2 Hz**, ending cleanly with `#end reason=complete rows=238`. That square is also where the robot
**measured itself**: the four turns gave an effective **track width of 95 mm**, replacing an assumed
value of 176 mm that had been in the code, and confirmed the **wheel diameter at 63.5 mm**. The same
data settled an earlier scare that the robot "spins left" — per-side heading drift came out under 1°
per foot, so it drives straight. `main.py`, the competition program, was **written for the first
time** — deliberately left unwritten until there was hardware evidence to write it against — then
code-reviewed with five fixes applied. The deploy toolchain was hardened the same day: uploads are
now auto-minified and split into 512-byte CRC-chained chunks because the hub rejects a 4096-byte
chunk, and a dependency resolver that reads the program's imports found that `main.py` actually needs
**fifteen** modules, two more than a hand-written list had caught. One result from this day was later
**overturned**: the chromaticity detector was validated at **0 false triggers in 238 rows** on that
floor, and five days later it was measured to fail outright on the real classroom carpet.
