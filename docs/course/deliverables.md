## Journal — 80 points · due 15 SEP

**The journal is worth more than the peer evaluations (80 vs 50) and more than the survey and peer eval
combined would suggest for the effort involved. It is the cheapest guaranteed score in the project:
it is scored on showing up and writing 8 real sentences, not on whether the robot works.**

Written in the **last 5–7 minutes of each class day**, independently, by hand, on the provided form.
Each day the instructor posts a **Question of the Day (QOD)** on the board in class.

### Rubric (verbatim structure, p.2 of the instructions; wording confirmed against the Canvas rubric)

| Criterion | Points | Deduction | Full credit means |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Journal entry each day? | **30** | −5 per missing entry | An entry exists for **all six** class days. Six entries. Miss two days and 30 becomes 20. |
| Entries clearly answer the question asked (not so general as to be meaningless) | **20** | −5 per violation | Every entry visibly answers **that day's posted QOD** — restate the question, then answer *it*, with specifics from that day. A generic "teamwork is important" paragraph scores this at 0 even if it is four sentences. |
| Entries more than trivial effort | **20** | −5 per violation | **≥4 sentences answering the QOD** AND **≥4 sentences summarizing the day's events**, **8 sentences total, minimum**. The count is per entry, and each short entry is its own −5. |
| Entry grammar/clarity | **10** | holistic | Complete sentences, correct spelling, legible handwriting. Canvas full-credit wording: "Entries free from grammar issues and are easy to read and understand." |

### What "full credit" concretely requires

Six handwritten entries, each with **DATE**, **NAME**, the QOD as posted, an answer of **at least four
sentences** that is unmistakably an answer to *that* question, and a summary of the day of **at least
four sentences** naming what actually happened — parts bought, code written, what broke, who did what.

`source-material/journal_about.html` (the Canvas assignment page) shows **"Ungraded, 50 Possible Points"** in

### ⚠ The provided example does not meet the bar

for color sensors."). That is below the ≥4-sentence summary bar — a −5 under the effort criterion.
**Treat the example as a guide to the form's layout, not to the required length.**

## Mid-Project Check-in Survey — 20 points · on/around 1 SEP

Completed in Canvas on the date Canvas indicates. It asks about your own process **and asks you to
assess your peers' effort and performance**.

Question types the instructions name (p.2) — these are the actual examples given, so expect them:

- "You have to fire someone, who would you fire?"
- "Whose roles would you switch?"
- "Who would you hire from another team? How many Schrute Bucks would you pay for them?"
- "What teamwork dynamics have worked well, what has been a challenge?"
- Reflection questions on **coordination/process**, **communication**, **individual motivation**, and
  **how this activity supported your learning**.

**`[ASSUMED]` full credit means: submitted, on time, in Canvas.** No rubric breakdown for the 20 points
appears in anything we hold, so "scored on completion" is an inference, not a stated rule.
The only realistic way to lose them all is to miss the Canvas due date — so check Canvas for the exact
date and time on 1 SEP rather than assuming it matches the calendar row.

**Practical:** the "who would you fire / switch / hire" questions are far easier to answer well if you
have kept a journal, because the journal already contains dated specifics about who did what. Write
from the journal, not from memory.

## Peer Evaluations — 50 points · due 15 SEP

A Canvas Quiz or Google Form completed after the project.

### Formula (p.3, verbatim)

```
Participation + Attitude + (Contribution * 8) = Total Score
```

Worked example from the instructions: Participation 7, Attitude 5, Contribution 8 →
`7 + 5 + (8 * 8) = 7 + 5 + 64 = 76`.

### Hard constraints

| Rule | Consequence of breaking it |
|---|---|
| Evaluate **ALL** team members, **including yourself** | An incomplete form is an incomplete submission |
| **Nobody can have the same Total Score** | Two identical totals is a rule violation; you must re-rank |
| Scores must be **whole numbers**, each 1–10 | Fractions are out of spec |
| You must total the last column yourself using the formula | Arithmetic errors are yours |
| Write **specifics** for anyone you score below a 75 total | Required by the instructions; a bare low score with no justification is not defensible |

**Note the weighting.** Contribution is multiplied by 8, so it is worth 8× either other axis. Maximum
possible total is `10 + 10 + 80 = 100`; minimum is `1 + 1 + 8 = 10`. Moving Contribution by one point
moves the total by 8; moving Attitude by one point moves it by 1. Because no two totals may tie and
Contribution dominates, **the practical way to break a tie is a ±1 on Participation or Attitude**, not
on Contribution.

### The 75 threshold

> "Average score for an individual below 75 can at the professor's discretion reduce the student's
> overall grade." (p.3)

75 is roughly `Contribution 8, Participation 7, Attitude 4` or `Contribution 8, Participation 6,
Attitude 5`. Anything with Contribution ≤ 7 lands at 56 + (P + A) ≤ 76 and is therefore near or below
the line almost regardless of the other two axes. Score honestly and objectively — the instructions
explicitly say not to let personal feelings sway the evaluation — but understand that a Contribution
score is close to being the whole grade you are handing someone.

### Definitions the instructions give (use these, not your own)

| Axis | Definition (p.3) |
|---|---|
| **Participation** | Attendance at meetings (in and outside class), punctuality to them, active participation in discussions and decisions, willingness to take on tasks. |
| **Contribution** | Actual completion of a "fair share" of tasks in a timely manner. |
| **Attitude** | Ability to work with other team members in a positive and enjoyable manner. |

Also see [team/roles.md](./team/roles.md) — teammates should know how they are being scored before the
project ends, not after.

## How to not lose easy points

1. **Never end a class day without writing the journal entry.** −5 each, six chances. It is the single
   largest cluster of guaranteed points in the project and it does not depend on the robot working.
2. **Count your sentences before you put the pen down.** Four for the QOD, four for the summary, eight
   total. Four short specific sentences beat two long vague ones — the rubric counts sentences.
3. **Restate the QOD in your first sentence** and answer *that* question. This is a separate 20-point
   criterion from effort; you can write eight excellent sentences and still lose it by drifting.
4. **Put names, numbers, and parts in the summary.** "Bought two motors (20 SB) and two wheels (14 SB);
   balance 56 SB" is specific and ungameable. "We made progress" is the failure mode the rubric names.
5. **Check Canvas for the survey's real due date on 1 SEP.** Twenty points that are lost only by
   forgetting.
6. **Do the peer evaluation arithmetic in a spreadsheet or with `python3 -c`,** then check that no two
   totals tie and every score is a whole number, before you submit. All four constraints are mechanical.
7. **Export the communications record early and repeatedly**, not on 15 SEP.
8. **Start the report from the repo, not from a blank page** — [report/outline.md](./report/outline.md)
   already says which file feeds which section.
9. **Watch the role rules while doing all of the above.** −2 Schrute Bucks per violation is a budget
   hit, not a grade hit, but the budget is what buys the robot. See [team/roles.md](./team/roles.md).
10. **Prefer writing to meeting.** Written digital communication is unlimited and free; face-to-face
    beyond the standup costs 1 SB per person per minute.
