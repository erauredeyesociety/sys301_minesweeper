## krupke2023-coverage-turn-costs

- **Authors:** Dominik Michael Krupke
- **Year:** 2023
- **Title:** Near-Optimal Coverage Path Planning with Turn Costs
- **Venue:** arXiv:2310.20340
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20340
- **Local:** `docs/research/papers/krupke2023-coverage-turn-costs.pdf` (gitignored) · sidecar `krupke2023-coverage-turn-costs.txt`
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-26
- **Relevance to the sweep problem:** Turns are this robot's weakest motion - a boustrophedon sweep does two point turns per lane, and each one spends time and injects heading error. This is the formal treatment of coverage with turn cost as a first-class objective.
- **Scope limit:** the paper does not prescribe long-axis lanes for a rectangle. What it shows is that raising the turn weight drives optimal tours toward longer straight lines and fewer turns (Fig. 2). Our long-axis choice is an inference from that trend and must be presented as ours, not as the paper's result.

- **Authors:** E. Fourney; J. W. Burdick; E. D. Rimon
- **Year:** 2024
- **Title:** Mobile Robot Sensory Coverage in 2-D Environments: An Optimization Approach with Efficiency Bounds
- **Venue:** arXiv:2405.15100
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15100
- **Local:** `docs/research/papers/fourney2024-sensory-coverage-efficiency-bounds.pdf` (gitignored) · sidecar `fourney2024-sensory-coverage-efficiency-bounds.txt`

## borenstein1995-umbmark-benchmark

- **Authors:** Johann Borenstein; Liqiang Feng
- **Year:** 1995
- **Title:** UMBmark: A Benchmark Test for Measuring Odometry Errors in Mobile Robots
- **Venue:** SPIE Conference on Mobile Robots, Philadelphia, 22-26 Oct 1995
- **Source:** https://johnloomis.org/ece445/topics/odometry/borenstein/paper60.pdf
- **Local:** `docs/research/papers/borenstein1995-umbmark-benchmark.pdf` (gitignored) · sidecar `borenstein1995-umbmark-benchmark.txt`
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-26
- **Relevance to the sweep problem:** The calibration procedure we intend to actually run (5 cw + 5 ccw square runs over a 4x4 m path, stopping at each corner). The most load-bearing paper in this corpus.
- **Do NOT cite this paper for the 10-22x figure.** Its Table I is "Summary of properties and UMBmark results for the six different vehicles tested" - a survey of six robots, with no before/after improvement column. The improvement table is in borenstein1995b (paper59).

## borenstein1995b-systematic-odometry-correction

- **Authors:** Johann Borenstein; Liqiang Feng
- **Year:** 1995
- **Title:** Correction of Systematic Odometry Errors in Mobile Robots
- **Venue:** IROS '95, Pittsburgh, 5-9 Aug 1995, pp. 569-574
- **Source:** https://johnloomis.org/ece445/topics/odometry/borenstein/paper59.pdf
- **Local:** `docs/research/papers/borenstein1995b-systematic-odometry-correction.pdf` (gitignored) · sidecar `borenstein1995b-systematic-odometry-correction.txt`
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-26
- **Relevance to the sweep problem:** The correction ALGEBRA behind UMBmark: the alpha/beta angles, Ed (unequal wheel diameters), Eb (wheelbase), and the cL/cR correction factors. paper60 says what to measure; this says what to do with it.
- **This is the source of the 10-22x claim, not paper60.** Table I, "Dead-reckoning Accuracy for Systematic Errors, Emax,syst, before and after compensation" (sidecar lines 585-620), lists eight experiments improving 15, 11, 10, 22, 12, 11, 21 and 19-fold. Caveat: experiment #7 reached 21-fold only after a SECOND compensation pass; its single-pass improvement was 6.4-fold. The paper's own summary claim is the more conservative "at least one order of magnitude".
- **Filename note:** keyed `borenstein1995b` because this paper is IROS '95, August 1995 - the same year as paper60. It is NOT the 1996 IEEE T-RA article "Measurement and correction of systematic odometry errors in mobile robots"; citing it as Borenstein & Feng 1996 would point a reader at a different publication.
